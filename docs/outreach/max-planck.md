# Email to the Max Planck LifeTrack team

Draft for Vane to send from her own address. English, short, no ask beyond "we exist", plus one gentle door. Replace the bracketed parts.

To: Wolfgang Fiedler (Max Planck Institute of Animal Behavior, Radolfzell), or the study contact listed on the Movebank study page for LifeTrack White Stork SW Germany

Subject: Your storks on a small, quiet website: storks.lessane.es

Dear Dr Fiedler,

My name is Vane. I live in Madrid and build small, non-commercial maps for fun. I wanted you to know about one I made with your data.

storks.lessane.es shows the public white storks of the LifeTrack studies (SW Germany and Loburg) as they move, read anonymously from Movebank's public endpoint every three hours. Underneath, it replays the CC0 archives you published on the Movebank Data Repository (SW Germany, Oberschwaben, Bavaria, Vorarlberg, Rheinland-Pfalz, and the MPIAB Argos set) as one slow year: every bird-year as a small comet, so visitors watch ten years of journeys at once, pouring over the Strait of Gibraltar in September.

Each bird carries the name your study gives it. Louis, from Radolfzell-Böhringen, is in a field near Lleida as I write this, and the page says so, with his birth nest and year taken from your reference data. Nothing is invented: where the data has no name or no place, the page shows none.

Every screen credits the studies, the authors and Movebank, with the citations from the repository. The site has no ads, no cookies and no tracking, and its code is open at github.com/lessane-land/storks.

If anything in it misrepresents your work, or if you would rather a bird or a study not appear, tell me and I will change it the same day.

And one small door, with no expectation: if there are more birds or studies you would be happy to see on a page like this, I would be glad to add them, with whatever delay or conditions you prefer.

Thank you for publishing the data so openly. It made this possible.

Kind regards,
